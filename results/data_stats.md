## What this means for the system

1. **Cap 2 is purely a selection problem.** Every item is exactly one tool call, over catalogs
   with a median of 105 tools and up to 283. Gold-tool recall of the shortlist
   ([tool_recall.md](tool_recall.md)) is therefore an upper bound on the Cap 2 score. In 12 of
   40 domains, even upstream's default of 128 tools shortlists.
2. **Cap 3 is almost always exactly 2 hops** (90%). A plan-then-act agent with two retrieval
   steps, one per hop, matches the data.
3. **Cap 1 needs long chains:** 60% of items need 3 or more BI operations. That is where state
   handling (data handles from one step feeding the next) matters most.
4. **Cap 4 is mostly API calls; documents are rare.** Only 3% of train turns (7% of dev-eval)
   call a document retriever, and always together with APIs.
   - The gold names retrievers `retriever_clapnq_<domain>`, but the live environment exposes
     them as `query_<domain>`. Scoring compares tool *responses*, so this is harmless, but
     tool-name matching in our diagnostics must map the two.
5. **Abstention is a policy problem.** All "I can not answer" turns sit in dialogues with a
   tool-use policy, and 96% of them make no tool call. The intended behaviour is to refuse
   when the policy forbids the tool the question needs. Upstream's scorer gives full credit for
   "unanswerable and no tool call", so a policy guard that abstains correctly is worth about
   8–9% of Cap 4 turns. That is V6 in the experiment matrix.
6. **Some Cap 4 dialogues cannot fit in context.** In `dev-eval`, 10 of 60 dialogues carry
   1.9–9.7 million characters of history, earlier turns' gold answers in `authors` and `menu`.
   That is far beyond the model's context window, whatever the tool count. Upstream's agent
   sends the full history, so these fail. Our runner should truncate long answers in the
   history.
